## Revised Phase Plan

---

### Phase 1 — Emergency: Fix the Leak (Week 1)
**Est. cost: USD 200–450**

The terrace and/or balcony are the most likely culprits for the corner leak. Losas sin membrana are the #1 source of water damage in Olavarría houses.

| Task | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Locate exact leak source | Roof-top inspection — check corner flashing, canaleta, and losa surface |
| Apply membrana asfáltica or líquida on affected losa area | Minimum 2 coats. Full terrace treatment if budget allows now |
| Re-seal canaleta corners | Replace union pieces or apply sellador de canaletas |
| Assess balcony waterproofing | Front balcony needs same check — do both while on scaffolding |

**Rule:** Don't proceed to any interior finish until zero leaks confirmed after rain.

---

### Phase 2 — Waterproofing All Exterior Surfaces (Weeks 2–4)
**Est. cost: USD 500–800**

Two floors means two sets of exterior exposures — losa, balcón, terraza all need attention before interior work begins.

| Task | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Full membrana treatment on rear terrace | This is the main flat roof — likely the source of leak |
| Front balcony waterproofing | Apply membrana or revestimiento hidrófugo |
| Check all exterior wall joints and window frames | Re-mortar and seal where cracked |
| Laundry room waterproofing | Often neglected — seal floor/wall junction |

---

### Phase 3 — Core Services: Electrical, Plumbing, Gas (Weeks 3–9)
**Est. cost: USD 2,000–2,800**

Two floors means longer runs for all services. Confirm what's already roughed in vs. what needs to be run from scratch.

#### Electrical
- Tablero with differential + thermal breakers (one per floor or combined panel)
- Circuits: lighting, outlets, AC drops (at least living and each bedroom), dedicated kitchen circuit
- Run all conduit before any floor tile or wall finish

#### Plumbing
- 2 bathrooms to fully rough-in (supply + drain)
- Kitchen sink and laundry connections
- Hot water: a single calefón or termotanque serving both floors — plan the location carefully (usually laundry room or near bathrooms)
- Confirm desagüe connects to municipal cloaca (España 3175 is in microcentro — should be connected)

#### Gas
- Run to kitchen and water heater
- Gasista matriculado required for certification
- Pressure test before closing walls

**Critical decision before Phase 3:** Walk the house with a plomero and electricista together. Two trades, one visit — they'll catch conflicts before they happen.

---

### Phase 4 — Both Bathrooms (Weeks 8–14)
**Est. cost: USD 2,000–2,800**

Two bathrooms is the most expensive part of this reno. Do them together to get better pricing on materials and labor.

| Task | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Waterproof both shower areas | Membrana líquida on walls and floor before any tile |
| Install toilets, sinks, shower/bañadera in both | Buy matching sets — you'll negotiate a better price on 2 |
| Tile both bathrooms — floor and walls | 30×30 or 30×60 porcelain. Budget ~15–20 m² tile per bathroom |
| Vanities and mirrors | Keep simple — plain mesada with pileta under-mount |
| Ventilation in both | Fan or operable window — mandatory |

**Budget tip:** Casa de sanitarios in Olavarría (there are several near the microcentro) will be 20–30% cheaper than buying at a cadena. Go there directly.

## Budget Reality Check

At USD 10k, the low estimate is reachable **if:**
- You manage materials purchasing yourself (never give a builder a materials budget)
- You do Phases 4 and 5 together to negotiate a combined tile/labor price
- You use Argentine-made sanitarios (Ferrum, FV, Bambi) rather than imported brands
- You get 3 quotes for every trade
- You defer any cosmetic upgrades (light fixtures, hardware) until after moving in

If you hit $10k and aren't done: **garage and patio can wait.** Prioritize habitability — the upper floor is what you'll live in.

## Parrilla Design — Back Terrace

### Layout
```
[Pergola overhead — full terrace width]
|                                          |
|  [PARRILLA]    [Dining table 4 pax]     |
|  brick + iron  wood table               |
|                                          |
|  [Potted plants along railing]           |
```

### Parrilla Specs
- **Structure**: Ladrillo visto (exposed brick), 80cm wide × 60cm deep × 90cm tall
- **Interior**: Lined with cemento refractario (heat-resistant mortar)
- **Grill**: 60×80cm plancha de hierro (10mm steel grate) — sits on adjustable iron supports at 3 heights
- **Chimney**: 15cm iron pipe, 1.5–2m tall — draws smoke up and away from pergola
- **Ash drawer**: Optional small opening at base for easy cleaning
- **Cost**: USD 300–500 materials + USD 200–300 labor = **USD 500–800 total**

### Pergola Specs
- **Posts**: 4 × quebracho or eucalipto tratado — 10×10cm, 2.4m tall
- **Beams**: 2 main beams running length of terrace
- **Slats**: 12–15 cross slats, 5×10cm, spaced 30cm apart — allows light through
- **Finish**: Teak oil or outdoor wood stain — dark walnut tone
- **String lights**: Hung along beams
- **Cost**: USD 400–600 materials + USD 200–300 labor = **USD 600–900 total**
